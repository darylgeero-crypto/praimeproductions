Record vocals over a beat — takes on lanes

Restored from the original Full Beats Vocal Studio. Load a beat, press Play beat, then Record while it plays. Wear headphones — the beat must come through headphones only, or it will bleed through speakers into your mic take. The mic is recorded only (it is not played live through speakers), so there is no feedback loop.

How takes are stamped: each take records the beat time it started at, plus the section you picked and the bar it falls on (bar = beat time ÷ (60 ÷ BPM × 4 beats), starting at bar 1). Takes are mixed at their recorded start time, so a take recorded over the hook stays over the hook in the mix.

Any-Mic Vocal Master — through the mastering channels

This is the core idea you started with: record on any microphone, in a quiet place, and run that take through the same mastering channels as a pro vocal — so the mic does not decide the quality. It gets you close to studio quality from a quiet room, not identical to a $3,000 mic in a treated room (see limits below).

Chain, in order: noise gate (quiets hiss between phrases) → high-pass 90 Hz (rumble out) → de-ess dip at 6.5 kHz → style EQ (low / presence / air) → compressor → gentle saturation → limiter at -1 dB → normalised to -1 dB peak. Style profiles come from the Styles page — pick one, or pick “From Styles page (bus)” and it uses the style you saved there.

Honest limits: EQ can compensate for a mic's frequency curve, compression can even out level, and a gate can quiet hiss — but it cannot fully undo a mic's self-noise, distortion from clipping, or room echo/reverb. Record close to the mic, do not clip, and record in the quietest room you have: a quiet closet with clothes around you beats a noisy kitchen with any plugin. If the take clips or echoes badly, re-record it — mastering cannot rescue that.
