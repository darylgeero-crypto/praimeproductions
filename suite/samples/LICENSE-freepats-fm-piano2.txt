AUTHENTICITY — multi-sampled DX7 electric piano, not a runtime oscillator
- The README states the samples were "Recorded from Hexter software
  synthesizer. Hexter is a free software program that accurately
  models the sound generation algorithm of the original instrument."
  So the source is a recording of a DX7 FM electric piano patch (a
  bright variation of the patch) rendered through the Hexter model —
  a real electronic instrument sound, captured as audio files, NOT a
  Web Audio oscillator generated at runtime in the app. The bank is
  labelled "FM Piano 2" honestly; it is not presented as an acoustic
  piano. Electric piano and electronic instruments are explicitly in
  the wanted list (keys / electronic), and this is the FreePats
  electric piano. The standing exclusion of the FreePats synth-*
  banks (synth-strings/brass/bass/pad) does not apply here: those are
  synth imitations of acoustic instruments, while the DX7 electric
  piano patch IS the electric piano itself — there is no acoustic
  original being faked. This is the same judgement as the fm-piano1
  run, for the same reason; FM Piano 2 is a DIFFERENT patch from FM
  Piano 1 (bright variation vs "E. Piano 1"), so it is a separate
  bank, not a duplicate.
- All 12 source FLACs are 44.1 kHz mono 24-bit; durations 7.65-29.91 s
  (long natural FM sustain, low notes ring longest).
- Pitch probe (measure_fmpiano2.py): 11 of 12 notes within -1..+5
  cents of keycenter (mean +1.9). F#1 (MIDI 30) reads +12 cents on the
  0.5 s harmonic-sum scorer, but a 2 s-window per-harmonic check puts
  its fundamental at -4.2 cents (h2 +0.5, h3 +1.6) — the +12 reading
  is pulled up by strong inharmonic FM sidebands at h4-h6 (+13..+16
  cents), not a mistuned fundamental. No correction applied (SFZ has
  no tune= opcodes). Sources peak at -0.05 dBFS, uniform across all 12.

  hang/bagpipe/tubular-bells/old-piano/fm-piano1 runs).
